**Numeric contradictions**

1. **Pixel error vs. translated range error don't scale consistently.** §5.1 reports the pixel-domain medians: "the predicted point lies a median of 20.6–22.1 px away in the three runs, against 52.6–59.8 px for the bottom-centre of the box." That gives a box-to-keypoint ratio of roughly 56/21 ≈ 2.6–2.8. But the same paragraph's translated range errors give "3.98 m at 15 m from a camera 1.5 m high and 1.99 m from a camera 3 m high; for the keypoint the values are 1.84 m and 0.92 m" — a ratio of 3.98/1.84 ≈ 1.99/0.92 ≈ 2.16. Eq. (5) is linear in δv at fixed D, h, f, so translating the *same* pair of pixel medians at two heights must preserve the pixel-domain ratio. It doesn't (2.6–2.8 vs. 2.16, a ~20–30% discrepancy). Either different δv values were silently substituted (e.g., the 94-tree blind-click numbers, 23 vs. 75.6 px, ratio 3.29 — even further off) or there is an arithmetic slip. This needs to be reconciled and the exact δv inputs stated.

**Overclaim contradicted by the paper's own evidence**

2. The Conclusion states the highway analysis "identifies hot, treeless stretches **near the two cities** as the first places to plant" (§7), echoing the abstract's "the hottest kilometres have the fewest trees." But §6.5 itself states: "along the rural middle of the route, temperature and tree density are unrelated (Spearman −0.02, p = 0.87, n = 99 stretches more than 30 km from either city)." Yet the new priority Table 5 — the very table meant to operationalise this "near the cities" claim — ranks km 42–43 (rank 13) and km 87–88 (rank 12) in its top 15, both well into the "rural middle" zone the paper says shows no temperature–density relationship, with 98–100% bootstrap stability driven entirely by zero detected trees rather than heat. The conclusion's geographic framing is not supported by, and is partly contradicted by, the table that is supposed to demonstrate it. This should be qualified ("mostly near the cities, but not exclusively") rather than stated flatly.

**Contradicts pre-specification disclosures**

3. §4.5 (Methods) describes exactly one VLM gap-filter threshold: "A spot is discarded if the score exceeds a threshold chosen on the validation area as the most precise threshold that keeps at least 80% of the truly free spots." §5.4 (Results) then introduces "a second, stricter threshold, fixed in advance as the most precise one on the validation area that keeps at least half of the truly free spots, raises the share of truly free candidates on the test area to 63.0%." This second rule is called "fixed in advance" but appears nowhere in the Methods description of the protocol, and is not listed among the three explicitly disclosed non-pre-specified exceptions in §1/§3.5 ("retraining the Pasadena detector…", "the depth model with a scale fitted on surveyed trees…", "the figures show highway densities as detected…"). A paper that otherwise insists every protocol element be dated and amendments numbered cannot introduce a second threshold in the Results and assert it was pre-registered without documenting it the same way — this is exactly the kind of after-the-fact rule the disclosure framework exists to flag, and as written it undermines the paper's own transparency claims.

**No new fatal issue identified** beyond these three; the core geometric and validation results (AP@4 m 0.640, field-distance errors, gap recovery) are internally consistent between abstract, tables and text.

**Verdict: major revision.**
