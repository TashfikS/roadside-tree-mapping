## (1) Fatal flaws

None that invalidate the paper outright, but one issue borders on fatal: the paper's central selling point is "every decision that could be tuned to the test data was fixed in a dated protocol file before the corresponding result existed" (§3.5). Yet §5.2 admits: *"the decision to retrain followed diagnostics that included the test area"* and *"the test area was scored for several versions... the first detector with its initial and its validation-chosen merge setting, the retrained detector, the depth model and the Mapillary photos."* Scoring a held-out set five-plus times while deciding whether to keep iterating is test-set leakage by the paper's own definition of the term used to criticize prior work (§1, "leakage... inflates scores"). The authors flag this as a limitation, which is honest, but it undercuts the paper's main methodological claim rather than merely qualifying it.

## (2) Major issues

- **Headline Pasadena AP@4m excludes half the test set.** §5.2: *"the 5,309 records flagged as difficult are ignored, leaving 5,353"* out of 10,662 total records. The reported 0.640 is computed on the "easy" half; under strict scoring (all records counted) it drops to 0.565 — a 12% relative decline not mentioned in the Abstract.
- **Evaluation harness is not actually uniform despite the stated claim.** Contribution 1 states *"all detectors are scored with one evaluator"* (§1) and Table 3's caption says *"every model scored by the same COCO evaluator."* But footnote (a) on the best-scoring model (Faster R-CNN, 0.652) says it was *"Scored with the same COCO evaluator inside its training job"* — a different invocation path than the other rows. The top result in the table is not obtained the same way as the rest, contradicting the "one evaluator" claim.
- **Keypoint ablation confound.** The pre-specified Pasadena ablation that concludes the trunk-base keypoint "does not improve" (in fact slightly lowers) the map score used keypoint labels that *"are generated with the same automatic Grounding DINO and SAM procedure... and are not reviewed"* (§4.1), whereas the Bangladesh keypoint evaluation that supports the opposite conclusion used reviewed/blind-clicked points. The two experiments measuring the same question used unequal-quality ground truth, yet both are reported with equal confidence.
- **Reference-point circularity and single annotator.** 88.6% of "reviewed" trunk-base points are "accepted automatic proposals" by the same model family being evaluated; the blind-click check used to counter this drew from only 200 trees, of which the detector found just 94 (47%) — a sizeable, unaddressed selection effect on the headline "23 vs 76 px" number. All annotation (including the blind clicks) comes from one annotator (§6.8), with no inter-rater check.
- **Undertrained baseline used for a strong narrative claim.** The retrained YOLOv4 baseline (0.544 AP50) that anchors the "leakage inflated prior results" argument *"stopped at iteration 1,888 of the configured 6,000"* (Table 3, footnote b) — the comparison supporting the paper's framing narrative used a truncated run.

## (3) Internal inconsistencies (quoted)

- Abstract: *"detectors reach 0.61–0.65 AP50"* vs. Table 3: Faster R-CNN 0.652 (above the stated upper bound of 0.65, and via the differently-scored harness noted above).
- Abstract reports only the lenient Pasadena score, *"the map scores AP@4 m 0.640"*, while §5.2 also reports *"AP@4 m is 0.565 (0.510–0.599)"* under strict scoring — the Abstract silently picks the higher number.
- §1 contribution 3 claims validation *"against the inventory and the city's planting-site records"* and Table 1 lists "Planting gaps: yes" for this study generally, but on the actual highway application (the paper's headline use case) §5.5 states *"no individual planting gaps are reported for the highway"* — the capability claimed in the framing sections is not delivered for the transfer test that titles the paper.
- §6.2 says the keypoint "gave no gain... and the score was slightly lower... (by 0.010 AP@4 m)" — consistent with §5.2's "−0.010" — but the Abstract compresses this to "it slightly lowers the map score," omitting that the stated 95% interval (−0.027 to −0.002) is wide relative to the point estimate, which the Discussion also doesn't flag as weak evidence despite emphasizing elsewhere (e.g., Pasadena AP) that intervals matter.

## (4) Overclaims

- Title and Abstract promise "validated... planting-gap maps... with a transfer test on the Dhaka–Chattogram highway," but the transfer test explicitly fails the pre-specified bar for gap-mapping (§5.5: *"the highway output is trees per kilometre, and no individual gaps are reported"*). The gap-validation machinery (§5.4) is never actually applied to the paper's titular case.
- §6.2's claim that 0.640 is *"comparable to or higher than"* Branson et al.'s 0.581 is not demonstrated to use the same "ignore difficult records" convention in both studies; without confirming this, the comparison is not established as apples-to-apples.
- Describing depth-network scale-fitting as recovering "comparable" performance (0.622 vs. 0.568–0.690 interval) overstates the practical takeaway, since the Discussion itself notes the fix needs *"surveyed trees,"* which is "exactly what is missing where an inventory is needed" — i.e., the comparison is not usable in the paper's own target deployment setting, yet is presented as a quantitative instance of near-parity.

## (5) Verdict

**Major revision.** The core geometric contribution and the discipline of pre-registered, per-output validation are genuine strengths, and most limitations are disclosed somewhere in the text. But the Abstract selectively reports the more favorable Pasadena metric, the evaluator-uniformity and label-quality claims underlying the keypoint/detector comparisons don't hold up under the paper's own footnotes, and the titular highway transfer test does not deliver the gap-validated product the framing promises. These must be reconciled — and the test-set reuse during model selection openly addressed — before acceptance.
